1 · What it is

Cars got super-sensors. Now people get one.

Self-driving cars see in every direction and never get distracted. People do not. We look at our phones, we focus on one thing, and we miss the box on the floor or the hand coming toward us. Spidey Sense adds one sense. It does not take over or decide for you. It keeps you in the loop and makes you a little more aware.

Pupil Labs Neon

Eye-tracking glasses. A scene camera plus eye cameras that measure where you look. They plug into an Android "Companion" phone, which streams video and gaze to a Mac over Wi-Fi.

DataFeel Dot

A coin-sized haptic device. It can vibrate, glow in any colour, and warm or cool. Dots chain to a hub called El Jefe. The Mac drives it over Bluetooth.

Mac + iPhone

The Mac runs the engine and calls Claude Haiku. The iPhone app shows and speaks why it buzzed, and lets you add triggers by talking. Optional: Oura ring heart rate via the cloud.

How it feels

You walk across a room reading a message. There is a box on the floor ahead. You have not looked at it.

About a second and a half later, the Dot buzzes amber.

Spidey's voice in your AirPods says why. You look up and step around it. The app logs it with the picture the glasses saw.

Now glance at the box first. Nothing happens. You already know it is there.

Your own triggers

Hold the talk button and say "warn me if a dog comes near me". Spidey drafts the trigger, you confirm it, and it is live.

Each trigger is a sentence plus a rule:

  • What: "something in my way", "someone about to attack me", "a lion I haven't noticed".
  • When: only if you haven't noticed it, when you look at it, or always.
  • Feel: vibration pattern, Dot colour, urgency.

2 · How it works

From sight to touch in about a second.

One Claude Haiku call per frame does all the looking. Your real gaze, not the model's guess, decides whether you noticed.

See

Neon streams the scene and your gaze point to the Mac over the phone's 5 GHz hotspot (~90 ms).

Ask

Every 0.25 s the newest frame, with the gaze dot drawn on it, goes to Claude Haiku. Up to 5 calls overlap.

Judge

One call checks the hazard check and every trigger. Each match gets a box, an urgency and a reason.

Noticed?

Did your gaze rest on that box (about 0.2 s)? A glance does not count. A gaze cache re-checks every frame.

Tap

If not noticed: the Dot buzzes over Bluetooth (117 ms), and the app speaks and logs why.

Every stage is swappable in backend/configs/*.yaml (fake or real source, detector and sink). Every Haiku call is traced: the exact images, prompt, reply, timings, and why each trigger did or did not fire.

Honest status

What works today, and what does not.

Works, tested live

  • Neon glasses → Mac over the Companion phone's hotspot.
  • Claude Haiku on live frames, with gaze-aware triggers: silent when you looked, buzz when you did not.
  • DataFeel Dot over Bluetooth: vibration patterns, a colour per trigger.
  • iOS app: event log with reasons and pictures, Spidey's voice, hold-to-talk triggers, step-by-step wizard.
  • Demo screen on the Mac with the app mirrored live.

Limits

  • Walking detection needs the motion stream. It works live (e.g. "Walking · 120 steps/min"), but if the glasses' IMU drops out, walking shows as "unknown" and never blocks a trigger.
  • The glasses microphone does not work today. The hearing code is built but gets no audio.
  • Oura heart rate is only as fresh as the last sync. Readings older than 15 min never buzz.
  • Radar is paused. Bluetooth must run from Terminal.app on the Mac.
  • No absolute distances: over-time triggers use relative signals (closer, farther, looming).

4 · Run it

Try it on a Mac.

Fakes need nothing but Python. Haiku needs an Anthropic key (kept in a gitignored .env). The Dot needs Bluetooth from Terminal.app. Full details are in the README.

Engine

./scripts/setup.sh          # venv + installs
source .venv/bin/activate
./scripts/set_api_key.sh    # Anthropic key → .env

# fakes only, prints cues
python3 backend/run.py --seconds 10
# Haiku on test scenes
python3 backend/run.py \
  --config backend/configs/config.vlm.yaml --seconds 21

Glasses + Dot (Terminal.app)

./scripts/ble_test.sh       # Dot flashes green
./scripts/neon_check.sh     # Neon fps / delay
python3 backend/run.py \
  --config backend/configs/config.neon.yaml \
  --sink datafeel-ble       # real glasses + buzz

./scripts/traces.sh         # viewer on :8096
# demo screen: http://localhost:8096/demo

iPhone app

Open ios/SpideySense/SpideySense.xcodeproj and press Run. The app finds the engine on the same Wi-Fi by itself (Bonjour). For the demo: Settings → Demo → Mirror screen to Mac.
